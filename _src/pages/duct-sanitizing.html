Clean First, Then Sanitize

Spraying a deodorizer into dirty ducts just masks the problem for a few days. The odor source, usually dust, dander, and organic debris, is still there. That's why we only apply sanitizing treatments after a full air duct cleaning has removed the buildup. Then the treatment can do its job on clean duct surfaces.

What We Use

We use EPA-registered products labeled for use in HVAC duct systems, applied according to the label directions. A fogging applicator distributes the treatment through the duct system while it's sealed off. Ask us about the specific product before your appointment if you have pets, small children, or chemical sensitivities, and we'll walk you through it.

A Note About Mold and Moisture

Middle Tennessee's humid summers can lead to condensation in crawl-space and attic ductwork. If you see visible growth in your ducts or smell a strong musty odor, the moisture source needs to be found and fixed first. That could be a leaking duct, poor insulation, a clogged condensate line, or an oversized AC unit. Cleaning and sanitizing won't stop growth from coming back if the moisture problem remains. We'll tell you honestly what we see during our duct inspection, and if your situation calls for professional mold testing or remediation, we'll say so.

Sanitizing FAQs

Is duct sanitizing safe for pets and kids?

We use EPA-registered products applied per label directions. We'll explain the product and any re-entry time before we start, and we're happy to skip the treatment if you prefer.

Will sanitizing get rid of smoke smell from a previous owner?

Cleaning plus sanitizing makes a big difference for smoke odor in ducts. Keep in mind that smoke also lives in walls, ceilings, and carpet, so ducts are one part of the solution.

Can you sanitize without cleaning first?

We don't recommend it. Treating dirty ducts masks the odor without removing the source, so we always clean first.
