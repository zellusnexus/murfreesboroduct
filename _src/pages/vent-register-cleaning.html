Why Vent Covers Get So Dirty

Supply registers push air into a room, and return grilles pull it back. Both collect a fuzzy layer of dust and pet hair on the louvers, and ceiling registers often develop dark "ghosting" streaks on the surrounding ceiling where dirty air hits the paint. A quick wipe doesn't reach between the fins or the backside of the damper.

What We Do

  • Remove each supply register and return grille
  • Wash the louvers, fins, and dampers to remove caked-on dust
  • Vacuum the boot (the opening behind the register) where debris collects
  • Check that dampers open and close freely
  • Reinstall covers securely and let you know about any that are bent, rusted, or worth replacing

Included With Whole-Home Duct Cleaning

Register and grille cleaning is part of every residential air duct cleaning we do. Cleaning the covers without cleaning the ducts behind them means they'll be dusty again in a matter of weeks, which is why we recommend doing both at once.

Return Grilles and Filter Grilles

Many Murfreesboro homes have a filter behind a large hallway or ceiling return grille. That return is usually the dirtiest spot in the whole system. While we're there, we clean the grille, the filter rack, and the return box behind it, and we'll tell you the correct filter size and how often to change it.

Vent Cleaning FAQs

Can I just clean the vent covers myself?

You can wipe or wash the covers yourself, and it helps. But the dust on your vents comes from inside the ducts, so it will return quickly unless the ductwork is cleaned too.

What are the dark marks on the ceiling around my vents?

That's usually dust and fine particles carried by the air stream and deposited on the ceiling. Cleaning the ducts and registers reduces it. Persistent marks can also point to air leaks around the register boot.
