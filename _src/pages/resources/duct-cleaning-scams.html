Why $49 Doesn't Add Up

A real, whole-home air duct cleaning means a crew of technicians spending several hours with truck-mounted or portable negative-pressure equipment, rotary brushes, and air whips, cleaning every supply run, every return, the trunk lines, and the return plenum. Between labor, equipment, fuel, and insurance, no legitimate company can do that for $49 or $99 and stay in business. So the low price has to be made up somewhere.

Common Red Flags

  • A "whole house" price that's far below every other quote you get
  • Robocalls, door-to-door, or mailers pushing a limited-time special
  • No firm price on the phone and no clarity on how many vents are included
  • Sudden "mold" or "contamination" findings once the crew is inside, with high-pressure upsells
  • Only vacuuming the register openings with a household or shop vacuum
  • No local address or a company name that keeps changing
  • Pressure to decide on the spot or "today only" pricing

Questions to Ask Before You Book

  1. What's the total price for my home, and what exactly does it include? Every supply and return? The trunk lines? The return plenum?
  2. How many vents does the price cover, and what's the charge per extra vent?
  3. What equipment do you use? Look for negative-pressure (high-powered, filtered vacuum) equipment plus mechanical agitation like brushes or air whips.
  4. Will you show me before & after photos?
  5. Do you follow industry cleaning standards? The National Air Duct Cleaners Association (NADCA) publishes the industry's cleaning standard. If a company claims NADCA membership, you can verify it on NADCA's website.
  6. Are you insured, and do you have a local address?

About "Mold" Upsells

Moisture problems in ductwork are real, especially in humid Middle Tennessee. But a technician can't confirm mold just by looking, and a legitimate company won't use it as a scare tactic to triple the price. If someone claims to find mold, ask to see it, ask for photos, and consider getting an independent opinion before agreeing to expensive add-ons.

Are all low-priced duct cleaning offers scams?

Not necessarily, but a very low "whole house" price should prompt questions. Ask exactly what's included and what extra charges might apply, and get the total in writing before work begins.

What should I do if a crew raises the price after arriving?

You're not obligated to proceed. Ask them to explain the increase, ask for photos of any problem they claim to find, and feel free to decline and get another quote.
