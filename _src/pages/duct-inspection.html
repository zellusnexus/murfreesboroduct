See Inside Your Ducts Before You Spend Money

Plenty of companies will tell every homeowner they need a cleaning. We'd rather show you. During an inspection, our technician looks at your return plenum, supply trunk, and several branch runs, then walks you through what we see. If your ducts look good, we'll tell you that.

What We Check

  • Debris level in supply and return ducts
  • Return plenum and filter rack, often the dirtiest part of the system
  • Construction debris in newly built or remodeled homes
  • Signs of moisture like condensation, rust, or water staining
  • Disconnected or crushed ducts in attics and crawl spaces
  • Filter fit, since gaps around the filter let dirty air bypass it

When an Inspection Makes Sense

  • You're buying or just bought a home and don't know the system's history
  • You're selling and want to know the condition ahead of a buyer's inspection
  • Someone in the house has new or worsening allergy symptoms
  • You notice a musty smell or dust blowing from vents
  • Some rooms never get comfortable no matter how you set the thermostat

What Happens After

If a cleaning is warranted, we'll give you a firm price on the spot. There's no pressure to book that day. If we spot something that's outside of duct cleaning, like a disconnected duct in the attic or a condensation issue, we'll point it out so you can have your HVAC contractor take a look.

Inspection FAQs

Will you always recommend a cleaning?

No. If your ducts are in good shape, we'll tell you and suggest when to check again. Our goal is to earn your trust, not to sell you something you don't need.

How long does an inspection take?

Usually 20 to 30 minutes for a typical home.
